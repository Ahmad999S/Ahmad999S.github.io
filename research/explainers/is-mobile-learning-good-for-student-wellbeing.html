Is mobile learning good for students' digital well-being?

Short answer

It cuts both ways. Mobile learning is flexible and engaging, but the reviewed studies also link it to distraction, anxiety, fatigue and shorter attention spans.

What the study did

A PRISMA-guided systematic review of 20 empirical and theoretical publications from 2015 to 2025, drawn from Scopus and Web of Science, supplemented with Telecom Regulatory Authority of India data on mobile use.

What it found

  • The review examines how digital well-being is defined and what affects screen time, multitasking, online behaviour, cognitive load and techno-stress.
  • Benefits include flexibility, engagement and individual learning patterns.
  • Risks include distraction, anxiety, fatigue and short attention spans.
  • The evidence has gaps in longitudinal studies, culturally diverse samples and intervention research.

Why it matters

The authors recommend teaching digital self-regulation skills, using adaptive interface design and setting institutional policies.

Limits

The review covers 20 publications, and the authors identify gaps in the underlying evidence.
