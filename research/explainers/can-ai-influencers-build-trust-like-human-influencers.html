Can AI influencers build the same trust as human influencers?

Short answer

In this study of TikTok users in Egypt and Jordan, AI influencers formed real emotional bonds with followers and were seen as credible. In some respects they did better than human influencers at building a sense of community and widening followers' networks. Human influencers reached similar results by a different route: storytelling depth and cultural resonance.

What the study did

The study compared followers' relationships with human and AI influencers using three theories: parasocial interaction (the one-sided bond a follower feels with a media figure), source credibility, and social capital. It traced how influencer type shapes emotional engagement, perceived trustworthiness, and two kinds of social capital: bonding (closer ties within a community) and bridging (links to new networks).

What it found

  • AI influencers can establish meaningful emotional bonds and credibility.
  • AI influencers sometimes outperformed human influencers in community cohesion and network expansion.
  • Human influencers relied more on narrative depth and cultural resonance to reach similar outcomes.

Why it matters

Brands in collectivist Middle Eastern markets cannot assume audiences will reject a virtual influencer. The route to trust differs, so strategy should match the influencer type and the cultural setting.

Limits

The authors call for measurement invariance testing and for longitudinal, mixed-method research before the findings are generalised.
