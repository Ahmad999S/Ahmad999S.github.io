Does information overload stop people from shopping sustainably?

Short answer

Yes, through decision fatigue. In two studies of Pakistani consumers, cognitive overload reduced sustainable purchase intentions because it left shoppers fatigued by decisions. How hard digital 'nudges' push also mattered: light nudging softened the effect, while heavy nudging made decision fatigue worse.

What the study did

Drawing on conservation of resources theory and dual-process theory, the authors ran a scenario-based experiment with 392 consumers in Karachi and Lahore (Study 1). They then used multiwave survey data from 214 online consumers in Pakistan (Study 2).

What it found

  • Cognitive overload substantially reduced sustainable intentions through decision fatigue (Study 1).
  • Study 2 confirmed the mediating role of decision fatigue.
  • Low-intensity digital nudging reduced the influence of cognitive overload.
  • High-intensity digital nudging increased decision fatigue, which harmed sustainable choices.

Why it matters

Retailers and platforms that want to encourage sustainable choices should simplify information and use gentle nudges rather than intense ones.

Limits

Both studies were conducted with consumers in Pakistan.
