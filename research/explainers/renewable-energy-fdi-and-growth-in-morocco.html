Does renewable energy drive economic growth in Morocco?

Short answer

Only in the short run, and the clearest effect was negative. Using data for 1993 to 2020, the study found a long-term equilibrium relationship among emissions, renewable energy, foreign direct investment, government green capital spending and growth. Green capital spending had a significant negative short-run correlation with growth. The long-run coefficients were not statistically significant.

What the study did

The authors applied an autoregressive distributed lag (ARDL) model within a Keynesian framework to annual World Development Indicators data for Morocco, with diagnostic and stability tests.

What it found

  • The bounds test confirmed a long-term equilibrium relationship among the variables.
  • Carbon dioxide emissions and renewable energy consumption showed positive trends.
  • Government green capital spending had a significant negative short-run correlation with economic growth.
  • Long-run coefficients were statistically insignificant.

Why it matters

Policy effects on growth may be weakened by wider macroeconomic structures, so green spending alone should not be expected to lift growth quickly.

Limits

The analysis uses national annual data for one country, 1993 to 2020.
