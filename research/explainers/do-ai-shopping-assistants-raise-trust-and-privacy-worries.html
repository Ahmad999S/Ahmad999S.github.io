Do AI shopping assistants make Gen Z trust online stores more, or worry more?

Short answer

Both. When open-source AI recommenders (such as DeepSeek) were more personalised and responsive, Gen Z shoppers trusted them more, but they also became more concerned about privacy. Trust and privacy concerns were both linked to higher purchase intention.

What the study did

The study used the Stimulus-Organism-Response framework and PLS-SEM to analyse survey data from 570 Gen Z online shoppers in Egypt, Saudi Arabia, Jordan and Qatar.

What it found

  • Interaction quality increased trust (β = 0.2585), AI familiarity (β = 0.2943) and privacy concerns (β = 0.5499).
  • Purchase intention rose with trust (β = 0.2848) and with privacy concerns (β = 0.3583).
  • AI familiarity had no direct effect on purchase intention (β = 0.0477).
  • The model explained 47% of the variance in purchase intention.

Why it matters

Better AI recommendations do not remove privacy worries; they raise them. The authors advise retailers to pair interaction quality with transparency and user control.

Limits

The findings come from a survey of Gen Z shoppers in four Middle Eastern countries.
