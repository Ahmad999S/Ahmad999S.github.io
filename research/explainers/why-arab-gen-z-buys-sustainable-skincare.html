Why do Arab Gen Z consumers buy sustainable skincare?

Short answer

Guilt about the environment is the link. Seeing yourself as a 'green' person raised purchase intention only through eco-guilt, not directly. Cultural values and green influencer marketing also shaped intention.

What the study did

An online survey in January 2026 gathered 369 adult responses (ages 18 to 29) from Egypt, Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates and Qatar. The authors extended the Theory of Planned Behaviour with green self-identity, eco-guilt, green influencer marketing, appearance-related social media consciousness and cultural orientation, and analysed the data with PLS-SEM in SmartPLS 4.

What it found

  • The model explains 78.3% of the variance in purchase intention, with strong out-of-sample predictive relevance (Q²predict = 0.734).
  • Green self-identity works entirely through eco-guilt (indirect effect 0.159, 95% CI 0.100 to 0.219).
  • Attitude, eco-guilt, green influencer marketing, vertical collectivism and both forms of individualism raised intention. Subjective norms had a significant negative effect. Perceived behavioural control and horizontal collectivism were not significant.
  • Appearance-focused social media consciousness weakened the effect of green influencer marketing, and vertical collectivism strengthened the link between identity and intention.

Why it matters

Sustainable beauty brands in Arab markets can use moral and emotional messages, choose influencers who build relationships rather than focus on appearance, and position products around family and duty.

Limits

The design is cross-sectional, so the authors note it does not permit causal inference.
