Has public education spending driven economic growth in Egypt?

Short answer

Not by volume alone. Over 1980 to 2023, physical capital was the strongest driver of long-run growth. Education spending showed a weakly negative long-run association, which the authors read as inefficient allocation rather than an absence of returns.

What the study did

The study used ARDL bounds testing with structural-break and stability tests, controlling for capital formation, labour participation, trade openness and foreign investment, and adding quality proxies such as completion rates and student–teacher ratios.

What it found

  • Long-run cointegration was confirmed (F = 6.23 against a 5% upper bound of 3.83).
  • The error-correction coefficient (−0.835) shows rapid adjustment to equilibrium.
  • Education spending had a weakly negative long-run association with growth (significant at 10%).
  • Quality proxies had a negative short-run effect and a positive effect after a one-period lag.

Why it matters

For Egypt Vision 2030 and SDGs 4 and 8, the study points to spending education money better rather than spending more.

Limits

This is a single-country time-series analysis.
